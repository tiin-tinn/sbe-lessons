Batik Pattern Scanner.

A mobile shopping app where young adults scan a batik pattern they like, learn what it means, and instantly browse matching Heritage Bay products.

01 / OVERVIEW

The thinking.

From the problem they found to the experience they designed.

01 / THE GAP

The challenge

The target audience has a short attention span and is easily frustrated by clumsy shopping interfaces.

Heritage Bay’s products can look expensive next to competitors’, so the experience has to add value — and product discovery isn’t personalised enough to help people find similar pieces.

02 / THE IDEA

The proposal

A pattern scanner lets users point their phone at a batik pattern they’ve seen, learn its cultural meaning and history, then browse matching or similar products straight away.

Personalised recommendations build on past scans and purchases, and a Save & Notify feature alerts users when an out-of-stock item returns.

03 / THE VALUE

Why it works

  • Culture is woven into the shopping journey rather than presented as extra reading.
  • Learning the story behind a motif adds value that helps justify the price.
  • A restrained palette with batik motifs as contextual backgrounds keeps the patterns centre stage.
02 / JOURNEY

How it unfolds.

The user’s path through the experience.

  1. 01Spot a patternThe persona sees a friend’s batik shirt and wants something similar.
  2. 02Open the appLog in and browse what’s new.
  3. 03Scan itPoint the scanner at the pattern.
  4. 04Learn the storyRead the motif’s meaning, history, care and specifications.
  5. 05Browse matchesSee products with the same or similar patterns.
  6. 06Save or buyAdd to cart, or save and get notified when it’s back.

FOR LEARNERS

What to look for.

  1. How a specific persona scenario — a pattern seen on a friend — grounds the scanner idea in a real moment.
  2. The emotion map, which shows exactly where the current journey dips.
  3. The rationale connecting each visual decision (colour, motif backgrounds, layout) to the brand.
