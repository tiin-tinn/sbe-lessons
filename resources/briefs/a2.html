BRAND EXPERIENCE DESIGN

WEIGHTAGE : 35%
Client Mox Monster
Preliminary release: sections relating to the client will be updated after the client briefing on 25 November. Deliverables, submission requirements and marking rubrics will not change.
Background

In Assignment 1, you developed a Brand Foundation System by creating your own creative agency, defining its positioning, belief, and identity.

Brands, however, are not only defined by how they look — they are shaped by how they are experienced.

Your agency has now been approached by a real client, Mox Monster, who is looking to strengthen its brand through a more meaningful and engaging experience.

Mox Monster is seeking to (to be confirmed at the client briefing on 25 November):

  • Create an engaging phygital experience for visitors to its new factory space
  • Turn factory visitors into fans who continue to engage with the brand after their visit
  • Develop experiences that reflect its brand personality and values

This assignment shifts your role from: Defining a brand → Designing how that brand is experienced

You will now take on the role of a design agency, applying your perspective to design a structured and intentional brand experience for a real client.
Client Context

Mox Monster is a Singapore toy studio and small-batch factory that designs and produces collectibles for brands, alongside its own original characters.

For this assignment, you will focus on Mox Monster’s new factory space, where its toys and collectibles are sculpted, moulded, painted and packed under one roof. Details of the factory space, including its key areas, visiting arrangements and current visitors, will be shared by the client at the briefing on 25 November, and this section will be updated accordingly.

Mox Monster does not currently have a formal brand guide. Draw your understanding of the brand from the client briefing, the client brief, and the brand assets on Brightspace. Work within the brand personality described by the client; you may extend its visual language where appropriate.
Objective

Design a focused Brand Experience that translates brand strategy into a clear and usable interaction.

You are required to:

  • Understand how users may discover, engage with, and experience the brand
  • Map a proposed customer experience journey for your selected opportunity
  • Identify key interaction moments and opportunities for engagement
  • Translate brand strategy into experience design
Task

Develop a Brand Experience Design for Mox Monster that supports the visitor journey for its new factory space.

Your proposed experience must include a clear digital interaction component that can be translated into a UX flow and clickable prototype.

Focus on identifying and proposing a meaningful brand experience opportunity that could strengthen how users interact with and experience the Mox Monster brand.

Your proposed experience should support at least one stage of the factory visitor journey: before the visit (discovery and booking), during the visit (in the factory space), or after the visit (continued engagement with the brand).

Examples may include:

  • Factory visit discovery and booking experience
  • “How a toy is made” station trail (e.g. scanning a QR code at each process area)
  • Make-your-own or painting workshop booking and companion experience
  • Custom toy order and production progress tracker
  • Character discovery or storytelling within the factory space
  • Digital stamp, collection or reward experience during the visit
  • Community engagement or loyalty experience after the visit

Use your journey map to decide where a digital interaction would add the most value. Select one key moment from your proposed journey and develop it in depth through your Experience Concept, UX Flow and Prototype.
Submission

Your submission should include:

A. Customer Journey Map

Mox Monster does not currently have a digital platform for its factory experience. Your journey map should therefore show both:

  • Current journey: how visitors discover, visit and engage with Mox Monster today, based on the client briefing and your own research
  • Proposed journey: how the same journey changes with your proposed experience, showing where your digital interaction is introduced

Your journey map should include:

  • Persona: A representative visitor to Mox Monster’s factory space, informed by the audiences described by the client and your own research. As the experience is new, your persona may include reasonable assumptions; state these clearly.
  • Key phases of the customer journey: The stages of the visit, for example: Discover → Plan → Arrive → Explore → Leave → Stay Connected. Use the same phases for both your current and proposed journeys so they can be compared.
  • User actions and interactions: Current: what the visitor does today and the touchpoints they use (e.g. Instagram, WhatsApp enquiries, walk-in visits). Proposed: where your new digital and physical touchpoints are introduced.
  • Thoughts and emotions: How the visitor thinks and feels at each phase. In the proposed journey, show how your experience is intended to change these.
  • Pain points and opportunities: Pain points in the current journey, grounded in the client briefing or your research, and the opportunities where your experience could add value.
  • Key moment: The one moment you will develop into your digital experience, clearly highlighted on the map, with a brief explanation of why you selected it.

Working with assumptions
Because your proposed experience does not yet exist, parts of your journey map will be based on assumptions. This is expected. Clearly distinguish between insights drawn from the client briefing or research and your own assumptions (for example, by labelling them), and ensure your assumptions are reasonable and consistent with what you know about Mox Monster and its visitors.

B. Experience Concept
Define your proposed experience:

  • Which key moment from your journey map are you designing for, and what focused brand experience or interaction does it require?
  • What problem or opportunity are you addressing?
  • How does this align with Mox Monster’s brand?
  • What insight or strategic perspective informs this approach?

C. UX Flow
Develop a UX flow that shows:

  • Step-by-step interaction process
  • Key decision points
  • Logical structure of the experience

D. Prototype & Interaction Design (CORE DELIVERABLE)
Translate your UX flow into a clickable prototype.

You may propose the most appropriate interface format for your experience (e.g. mobile, desktop, tablet, kiosk, or hybrid interfaces), depending on the nature of your proposed interaction and user context.

The selected interface format should be appropriate to the intended experience, interaction context, and user journey.

The prototype should be developed at a medium- to high-fidelity level, with sufficient visual and interaction detail to clearly communicate the intended user experience.

You are not expected to produce production-ready interfaces, but the prototype should demonstrate thoughtful interaction, usability, visual coherence, and clear application of UX/UI principles.

Your prototype must:

  • Be created using Figma
  • Demonstrate a clear and complete interaction flow
  • Include key screens required to understand the experience
  • Show navigation, transitions, feedback, and application of Don Norman design principles

Minimum Requirement:

  • At least 1 complete user flow
  • At least 5–8 key screens
  • Clickable navigation between screens

E. Rationale
Provide a clear explanation of:

  • Your design decisions
  • How the experience reflects the brand

Submission

  • Create your work using Figma
  • Export and submit as a PDF
  • Include a link to your prototype
  • Submit both via Brightspace
The goals of this assignment are to help you
  • Translate brand strategy into clear, meaningful, and usable brand experience
  • Apply CX, UX, and UI principles in a structured way
  • Design coherent and usable interaction flows
  • Develop working prototypes that demonstrate interaction thinking

Your solution should focus on a clear digital experience. You may explore how this could extend into the physical factory space and other touchpoints in Assignment 3.
